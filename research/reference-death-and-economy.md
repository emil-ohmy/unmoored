# How Death's Door and Tunic handle death, currency, healing and checkpoints

Research for ticket `.scratch/game-brief/issues/02-reference-death-and-economy-research.md`.
Gathered 2026-09-27. Sources are the game wikis (Death's Door Fandom wiki; for Tunic, the community
wiki at tunic.wiki, which transcribes the in-game instruction booklet, plus the Tunic Fandom wiki), Wikipedia and
published reviews. tunic.wiki.gg would not load (it served a bot check), so it was not used.

**A correction to the question first.** The question assumes neither game relies on stat upgrades. Both
games do sell stat upgrades for currency, just on a small scale:

- Death's Door: Soul Energy buys five levels each of Strength, Dexterity, Haste and Magic
  ([Abilities](https://deaths-door-game.fandom.com/wiki/Abilities)).
- Tunic: coins plus an "offering" item buy levels of ATT, DEF, POTION, HP, SP and MP
  ([Check-Point](https://tunic.wiki/books/items/page/check-point)).

Neither game has the open-ended grinding of Dark Souls. Both cap these upgrades, and in Tunic the offering
items are finite and found in chests. What the currency buys is still stats, though.

## Death's Door

### Death: what is lost and how it's retrieved
- **Nothing is lost.** You respawn at the last door you activated or used, with no other loss of progress
  ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Death%27s_Door_(video_game));
  [Hardcore Gamer](https://hardcoregamer.com/reviews/review-deaths-door/407474/)).
- You keep all your souls, so there is nothing to retrieve
  ([Nintendo World Report](http://www.nintendoworldreport.com/review/59014/deaths-door-switch-review);
  [Gamers Heroes](https://www.gamersheroes.com/honest-game-reviews/deaths-door-review/)).
- The real cost is the distance back to where you died. World changes such as ladders, opened doors and
  destroyed stones stay permanent, which shortens the return trip
  ([Hardcore Gamer](https://hardcoregamer.com/reviews/review-deaths-door/407474/)).

### Currency (Soul Energy)
- **Where it comes from:**
  - Enemies drop it: 1–16 souls from ordinary enemies and 100–700 from bosses.
  - Hidden "masses" of 100 souls are placed around the world, usually behind a small puzzle.
  ([Soul Energy](https://deaths-door-game.fandom.com/wiki/Soul_Energy))
- **What it's for:** mainly levelling the four Abilities at the Soul Vault. Each has five levels, costing
  400, 600, 800, 1000 and then 1500
  ([Abilities](https://deaths-door-game.fandom.com/wiki/Abilities); also
  [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Death%27s_Door_(video_game))).
  - Strength: melee damage and reach.
  - Dexterity: charge time and combo speed.
  - Haste: movement and dodge speed.
  - Magic: spell damage.
- **What it can't buy:** max HP. You have to find it
  ([Gamers Heroes](https://www.gamersheroes.com/honest-game-reviews/deaths-door-review/)).
- **Unclear:** Hardcore Gamer says souls also pay for fast travel. Neither the wiki nor Wikipedia mentions
  this, so it is unconfirmed
  ([Hardcore Gamer](https://hardcoregamer.com/reviews/review-deaths-door/407474/)).

### Healing
- **No carried heals.** You find Life Seeds and plant them in Green Pots placed around the world. A pot grows
  a Soul Fruit that restores you to full health when eaten
  ([Life Seeds](https://deaths-door-game.fandom.com/wiki/Life_Seeds);
  [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Death%27s_Door_(video_game))).
- Pots usually sit before big fights or bosses. There are 50 seeds and 50 pots in the game
  ([Life Seeds](https://deaths-door-game.fandom.com/wiki/Life_Seeds)).
- **Planting is a choice:** a seed is permanent once planted, and seeds are finite. Hardcore Gamer compares
  them to Resident Evil's ink ribbons
  ([Hardcore Gamer](https://hardcoregamer.com/reviews/review-deaths-door/407474/)).
- **Sources disagree on when fruit regrows:**
  - The wiki says it regrows when you enter a new area or die
    ([Life Seeds](https://deaths-door-game.fandom.com/wiki/Life_Seeds)).
  - Hardcore Gamer says only when you use a door
    ([Hardcore Gamer](https://hardcoregamer.com/reviews/review-deaths-door/407474/)).
- Going back to the hub refills your life
  ([Hardcore Gamer](https://hardcoregamer.com/reviews/review-deaths-door/407474/)).
- **Magic** isn't a potion resource. It refills when your melee hits land
  ([Weapons](https://deaths-door-game.fandom.com/wiki/Weapons);
  [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Death%27s_Door_(video_game))).

### Checkpoints and enemy respawn
- **Doors are the checkpoints.** Each area has a door you unlock, and each works both as a save point and
  as a portal back to the hub
  ([Doors](https://deaths-door-game.fandom.com/wiki/Doors)).
- **Enemies respawn.**
  - Hardcore Gamer says all enemies respawn whenever you use a door
    ([Hardcore Gamer](https://hardcoregamer.com/reviews/review-deaths-door/407474/)).
  - Gamers Heroes says they respawn when you die
    ([Gamers Heroes](https://www.gamersheroes.com/honest-game-reviews/deaths-door-review/)).
  - These fit together: dying sends you through a door.
- **How far apart they are:** TheGamer calls doors "relatively frequent" compared with Tunic's checkpoints
  ([TheGamer](https://www.thegamer.com/tunic-deaths-door-game-comparison/)).

### Equipment and upgrades: how they're found
- **Weapons:** you start with the Reaper's Sword. Four more are found in the world, some behind puzzles:
  - Thunder Hammer, in the Mushroom Dungeon.
  - Rogue Daggers, in the Estate of the Urn Witch.
  - Reaper's Greatsword, in the Stranded Sailor area.
  - Discarded Umbrella, at the hub.

  Each weapon differs in damage, number of swings, range and swing time
  ([Weapons](https://deaths-door-game.fandom.com/wiki/Weapons);
  [Items](https://deaths-door-game.fandom.com/wiki/Items)).
- **Spells:** you start with the Bow. You earn Fire, Bomb and Hookshot by beating an "Avarice" boss behind
  each ancient door. These spells double as the game's traversal keys: fire burns webs, bombs break walls and
  the hookshot crosses gaps. Beating an optional miniboss (a Silent Servant) upgrades each spell's behaviour,
  for example a burn effect or no self-damage from bombs
  ([Spells](https://deaths-door-game.fandom.com/wiki/Spells)).
- **Max HP and max magic:** there are 16 shrines, each holding one shard. Four shards of one colour add one
  unit to that bar, so you get two extra units of each at most
  ([Shrines](https://deaths-door-game.fandom.com/wiki/Shrines);
  [Items](https://deaths-door-game.fandom.com/wiki/Items)).

## Tunic

### Death: what is lost and how it's retrieved
- **You drop coins where you die** and leave an "echo-of-self" ghost there. Touch the ghost to get the coins
  back
  ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tunic_(video_game));
  [booklet p.19, tunic.wiki](https://tunic.wiki/books/instruction-booklet/page/page-19-death)).
- Touching the ghost also lets off a small burst that stuns nearby enemies. This can make dying into a
  tactical option
  ([GGRecon](https://www.ggrecon.com/reviews/tunic-review/)).
- Two cards change the echo: Louder Echo gives a bigger stun, and Magic Echo restores MP when you collect it
  ([Cards](https://tunic.fandom.com/wiki/Cards)).
- If you die again before collecting it, the dropped coins are gone for good
  ([Pro Game Guides, via search excerpt](https://progameguides.com/tunic/how-to-recover-your-items-in-tunic/)).
  I could not load the page to confirm the wording, and no wiki states this.
- **How much you drop is unconfirmed. Sources disagree:**
  - Wikipedia and GGRecon say "some" or "a number" of your coins, and GGRecon adds "you don't lose much"
    ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tunic_(video_game));
    [GGRecon](https://www.ggrecon.com/reviews/tunic-review/)).
  - TheGamer says all the coins you carry
    ([TheGamer](https://www.thegamer.com/tunic-deaths-door-game-comparison/)).
  - Players on Steam report about 20 coins
    ([Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/553420/discussions/0/604143843941653651/)).

  The consensus leans towards a small, partial loss, but I found no wiki or developer source that settles it.

### Currency (coins, which the game calls "gems")
- **Where it comes from:** enemy drops, many of the 245 treasure chests, and Effigy consumables. An Effigy's
  payout doubles with each one you break, up to 512 coins
  ([Treasure Chests](https://tunic.wiki/books/items/page/treasure-chests);
  [Items](https://tunic.fandom.com/wiki/Items)).
- **What it's for:**
  1. **Stat offerings at checkpoints.** You pay coins plus one offering item to gain one level in a stat.
     The six items each raise a different stat:

     | Offering item | Stat raised |
     | --- | --- |
     | Tooth | ATT |
     | Effigy Statue | DEF |
     | Ash | healing per potion |
     | Flower | max HP |
     | Feather | max SP |
     | Mushroom | max MP |

     The offering items are finite and found in chests, so a normal playthrough caps the stats. Each level
     costs more, typically 100 to 450 coins
     ([Check-Point](https://tunic.wiki/books/items/page/check-point);
     [Offerings](https://tunic.fandom.com/wiki/Offerings)). The two wikis name the items slightly differently.
  2. **Hidden shops.** They sell bombs, fruit, lures and Ivy. They also sell two extra potion flasks, for 300
     and 1000 coins, and two Golden Coins at 999 each. What's on sale grows as you progress
     ([Spectral Seller](https://tunic.wiki/books/creatures/page/spectral-seller)).
  3. **Golden Coins become card slots.** You wish at a well, and slots unlock after 3, 6, 10 and 15 wishes
     ([Items](https://tunic.fandom.com/wiki/Items)).

### Healing
- **Magic Potion flasks are the main heal.** They "mysteriously refill whenever you touch a CHECK-POINT"
  ([booklet p.17](https://tunic.wiki/books/instruction-booklet/page/page-17-items);
  [Items](https://tunic.fandom.com/wiki/Items)).
- **Getting more flasks:**
  - Find potion shards: "Find some and you get a free flask". There are 12 shards per playthrough.
  - Find whole flasks, or buy them.
  ([booklet p.17](https://tunic.wiki/books/instruction-booklet/page/page-17-items);
  [Collectables](https://tunic.wiki/books/items/page/collectables))
- **Making each flask heal more:** offer Ash
  ([booklet p.26](https://tunic.wiki/books/instruction-booklet/page/page-26-survival-tips)).
- **Other healing:**
  - Red and Blue Fruit are consumables that restore HP or MP and are "gone forever" once eaten
    ([booklet p.17](https://tunic.wiki/books/instruction-booklet/page/page-17-items)).
  - The Lucky Cup card gives enemies a 15% chance to drop a small heart
    ([Cards](https://tunic.fandom.com/wiki/Cards)).
- **Magic** refills when you rest, and some enemies drop soul pickups that restore it
  ([booklet p.17](https://tunic.wiki/books/instruction-booklet/page/page-17-items);
  [booklet p.26](https://tunic.wiki/books/instruction-booklet/page/page-26-survival-tips)).

### Checkpoints and enemy respawn
- **What a checkpoint does:** interacting with one fully restores HP, SP, MP and potions. It also resets all
  "respawnable" enemies, but not bosses. You respawn at the last checkpoint you set
  ([Check-Point](https://tunic.wiki/books/items/page/check-point);
  [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tunic_(video_game))).
- **Offerings:** checkpoints are also where you spend offerings
  ([Check-Point](https://tunic.wiki/books/items/page/check-point)).
- **How far apart they are:** checkpoints are sparser than in Death's Door, "so each death really does feel
  like a setback"
  ([TheGamer](https://www.thegamer.com/tunic-deaths-door-game-comparison/)).

### Equipment and upgrades: how they're found
- **Found in the world, almost all from chests** (245 in total)
  ([Treasure Chests](https://tunic.wiki/books/items/page/treasure-chests)):
  - Stick, then Sword.
  - Shield, which blocks at a stamina cost.
  - Magic items: the Magic Dagger (ice), the Wand, the Magic Orb (a grapple that pulls you or enemies), the
    Gun and the Hourglass.
  - Traversal gear: the Lantern, and the Hero's Laurels, which replace the dodge roll with a short teleport
    that crosses gaps.
  ([Items](https://tunic.fandom.com/wiki/Items);
  [Collectables](https://tunic.wiki/books/items/page/collectables))
- **Cards** are passive modifiers you equip in limited slots. There are 16 of them, placed in the world.
  Examples:
  - Parry window up.
  - Double i-frames when dodging.
  - Tincture: ATT up, DEF down.
  - Flame Sword: HP set to 1, but sword hits set enemies on fire.
  - Inverted Ash: potions restore MP instead.
  ([Cards](https://tunic.fandom.com/wiki/Cards))
- **Offerings:** as described under Currency.

## Patterns that fit abilities + equipment, no stat upgrades

The two games split the death-and-economy problem differently:

| | Death's Door | Tunic |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Death cost | Distance back to the fight | Distance back, plus a small currency drop |
| Healing | Placed in the world | Carried, refills at checkpoints |
| Currency buys | Stats | Stats and consumables |

Neither pattern depends on its stat purchases. The options below keep the parts that fit this game and swap
out the stat purchases.

**Note for the brief.** Both references respawn enemies when you checkpoint or die. Charting marked
"checkpoints with enemy respawn" as *not picked*, so that choice departs from both references, and the
death penalty has to come from somewhere else. The options below say where.

1. **Death costs time, not currency (Death's Door model).**
   - Keep everything on death and respawn at the last checkpoint. The penalty is walking back.
   - Shortcuts that stay open and checkpoints spaced for tension set how hard it is.
   - *For:*
     - Simplest to build.
     - The currency design stays separate from death.
     - Suits a 1–2 hour game.
   - *Against:*
     - Weak death penalty. Tension depends entirely on how far apart the checkpoints are.
     - Without enemy respawn, the walk back is empty, so the penalty is nearly nothing.
     - The brief's "a death penalty" leaning would be only thinly met.
2. **Drop and retrieve with a bonus on pickup (Tunic's echo).**
   - On death, drop part (or all) of your currency as an echo. You get one chance to recover it, and a
     second death loses it.
   - Picking it up does something useful, such as a stun burst.
   - *For:*
     - A real soulslike stake.
     - Turns the run back into a small objective.
     - The bonus softens frustration.
     - Cheap to build: one persistent pickup per save.
   - *Against:*
     - Only works if currency matters (see option 3).
     - Without enemy respawn, the only risk on the way back is traps and enemies still alive in rooms you
       didn't clear.
     - Tuning question: how much to drop. Tunic seems to drop little, and reviewers said it "doesn't really
       raise the stakes" ([GGRecon](https://www.ggrecon.com/reviews/tunic-review/)).
3. **Currency buys equipment, equipment slots and consumables, never stats.**
   - Spend currency on things like:
     - Shop items: equipment variants, bombs and similar tools.
     - An extra slot for passive equipment, like Tunic's Golden Coin to card slot route.
     - An extra Healing charge, like Tunic's buyable potion flasks.
   - Keep weapons and Abilities as found rewards, not purchases, as both references do.
   - *For:*
     - Currency feels worth having without stat creep.
     - Pairs well with option 2.
   - *Against:*
     - Needs a shop or vendor and something worth buying at each point in a short game.
     - Too little stock and the currency feels pointless.
     - Too much and the shop replaces exploration as the source of rewards.
4. **Carried Healing charges that refill at checkpoints, with extra charges found (Tunic's flask).**
   - Carry a fixed number of charges, all refilled at checkpoints.
   - Extra charges come from shards or whole items found by exploring, not bought with stats.
   - *For:*
     - Matches the "limited charges" leaning and the `Healing charge` term in CONTEXT.md.
     - Clear rules.
     - Exploration rewards that aren't stats.
   - *Against:*
     - Each added charge is, in effect, a found max-heal upgrade. The brief should say whether that counts
       as a "stat upgrade". It is permanent, but found, not bought.
   - *Alternative, Death's Door's placed pots:*
     - They give the designer precise control over the healing before each fight.
     - They remove carried resource management.
     - Planting seeds adds a choice about where.
     - Heavier level-design work.
5. **Passive modifiers as equipment instead of stats (Tunic's cards, Death's Door's weapon profiles).**
   - Make the "equipment" layer a set of found items that each change how a verb behaves, rather than
     raising a number. Examples:
     - A longer parry window.
     - Faster dodge recovery.
     - Echo pickups that restore a resource.
     - A trade-off such as Tunic's Tincture or Flame Sword.
   - Or weapons that differ in profile (range, speed, swing count) rather than tier, like Death's Door's
     five weapons.
   - *For:*
     - Progression reads as new options, not bigger numbers, which fits "no stat upgrades" best.
     - Trade-off items give players choices instead of strict upgrades.
   - *Against:*
     - Each modifier needs its own code and balancing.
     - With only a few hours a week, 4–6 modifiers is probably the realistic ceiling.
     - Slot limits need their own UI.

**Likely combination to test in tickets 03/04:** options 1 or 2 for death, 3 and 5 for currency and
equipment, and 4 for healing. The open design question is whether a found max-health or charge upgrade
counts as a "stat". Both references rely on one, via shards in Death's Door and flowers and potion shards in
Tunic.
