# Hook, protagonist and pillars
Type: grilling
Status: resolved
Blocked by:

## Question

What is the game about, in one sentence (the hook), who is the protagonist, and which three pillars
(one line each) does every later decision get judged against? Must fit the melancholic-whimsical tone,
Death's Door/Tunic references, and what Claude can build in Blender.

## Answer

Resolved 2026-09-27 with the user (quiz format).

- **Concept**: The Ferryman's Apprentice.
- **Pitch**: "The dead have stopped crossing. Take up your vanished master's oar (your weapon, your tool,
  your way across the water) and find out what he's been keeping from them."
- **Protagonist**: the Ferryman's Apprentice, a small masked creature with an oversized oar.
- **Setting**: a misty, flooded underworld of islands; melancholic-whimsical, death as everyday routine.
- **Mystery**: the Ferryman stopped letting souls go and started hoarding them. He is the final boss; the
  Apprentice inherits his role.
- **Theme**: letting go. Souls have unfinished business the Apprentice helps them release; in the end the
  Apprentice lets the Ferryman go too.
- **Pillars**:
  1. **One oar, many uses**: every Ability is a new oar technique with a combat and a traversal use; the oar is the only weapon.
  2. **Deliberate, fair combat**: stamina, readable telegraphs, few but dangerous enemies; every death is the player's fault.
  3. **A world that folds back**: islands and paths loop back through shortcuts, water routes and secrets.
- **Primary hook**: the oar. The boat and the mystery support it.
- **Boat (light)**: the Apprentice rows between islands with the oar; no combat on the water; some Ability gates
  are on water (currents, fog, shallows).
- **Equipment**: oar attachments (change reach/speed/weight) + passive charms. No other weapons. This overrides
  the charting leaning "abilities + equipment" where equipment could mean other weapons.
- Considered and not chosen: the soul procession hook (souls trailing you, banked at jetties); a full boat mechanic.
