# Souls with unfinished business
Type: grilling
Status: resolved
Blocked by: 03

## Question

How does "letting go" show up in play? What do the NPC souls with unfinished business want, how does the
Apprentice help release them (a quest, a delivery, a fight, a choice), what does releasing one give the player,
and roughly how many does the brief commit to? Must not overlap confusingly with whatever currency turns out to be.

From the currency ticket: Obols can pay passage for souls, competing with buying gear. Decide whether paying a
soul's fare is optional (a moral/reward choice) or sometimes required for progress, and what the Apprentice gets back.

## Answer

Resolved 2026-09-27 with the user (quiz format).

- **Terms**: Soul = any of the dead. **Lingerer** = a soul with unfinished business (NPC). **Soured** = a soul kept too
  long by the Ferryman, now hostile (the enemies).
- **Count**: 5–7 Lingerers, about 2 per Area plus a couple in the hub.
- **Unfinished business varies** per Lingerer: a lost keepsake, a message to deliver to another soul, or something
  to face (a special encounter). The user explicitly wants variety rather than one quest type.
- **Crossing needs both**: resolve their business, THEN pay their fare in Obols. Paying is always optional;
  no Lingerer is required for progress. They wait until the Apprentice can afford it.
- **The Crossing**: the Lingerer waits at the hub jetty; the Apprentice rows them to the far shore in a short,
  calm, playable sequence carrying their last lines.
- **Reward**: a parting gift (Charm, Oar attachment or found upgrade piece) plus a memory of the Ferryman. The
  Lingerers are how the mystery is told.
- **Soured and respawn**: defeating a Soured only scatters it (it drops its fare as Obols); the Ferryman's hoard
  pulls it back, which is why enemies re-form on Rest/death. When the Ferryman falls, all are released at once.
- **Implication for world structure**: there is a hub with a jetty that Crossings leave from.
